About

I'm an M.S. candidate in Genome Science and Technology at the University of Tennessee – Oak Ridge National Laboratory Bredesen Center, where I lead an independent project on the epigenetic consequences of low-dose radiation exposure in human lung fibroblasts and epithelial cells, profiling H3K27me3, H3K9me3 and H3K27ac by CUT&RUN and integrating the results with RNA-seq and Hi-C.

Before joining UT, I spent two and a half years as a Junior Research Fellow at the L V Prasad Eye Institute, where I built the xeno-free expansion protocol for human limbus-derived mesenchymal stem cells, qualified the cell product under GMP conditions, and supported patient enrollment for clinical trial NCT04932629. That work produced a first-author paper in Stem Cell Research & Therapy and six co-authored articles.

What I bring to a biotech team: I can take a therapeutic hypothesis from benchtop molecular assay → nanoparticle formulation → in vivo efficacy study → regulatory-grade documentation, and analyze the genomics myself on HPC rather than handing it off. I'm an invited speaker at the 2026 Gordon Research Seminar and an Outstanding Scholar Awardee of the Radiation Research Society.

Low-Dose Radiation Epigenomics
